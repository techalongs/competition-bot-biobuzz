**Question**

Robot A has a mechanism that extends a bar or plate (Within the expansion limits according to R105.A) above the shooting mechanism of Robot B. When Robot B goes to launch their scoring elements, the game pieces are blocked by the extended plate of Robot A. Would this be a violation of any of the following: G407 (STRATEGIC possession of >4 scoring elements, if Robot A already had game pieces in their robot), Section 1.5.1 (Spirit of the competition), or G408 (if Robot A were to block an opposing NECTAR shot by Robot B)?

**Answer**

We cannot comment absolutely on specific ROBOT designs or scenarios. Blocking or defending shots taken are not specifically disallowed by the BIOBUZZ rules and the action of blocking a shot is not specifically a violation of the Spirit of the Competition.

If the ROBOT action meets the definition of CONTROL (as described in Section 16) then the ROBOT may be at risk of violating G407 or G408 while interacting with SCORING ELEMENTS.

## Q3: Over possession
<!-- qa:3 modified:1791609226000 answer:1791609226000 -->

**Question**

A team accidentally intakes a fifth game object, and is obviously trying to get rid of it from their robot. They manage to do so within 3 seconds, is this a penalty? (It is not strategic)

If the team takes longer than 3 seconds, is it then a penalty?

Do these rules change it the game object in question is an opposing alliance's Nectar?

**Answer**

Yes. Per G407, CONTROL of a fifth game object is a violation, and there is no MOMENTARY exception. The violation would be a VERBAL WARNING of the REFEREES perceive the action as non-STRATEGIC, but the greater penalty may apply if the actions are deemed to be STRATEGIC. We cannot comment absolutely on hypothetical scenarios. The ultimate decision would be determined by the REFEREES at your event, with the final call made by the Head REFEREE. Per the G407 Orange Box, A ROBOT that MOMENTARILY CONTROLS 5 SCORING ELEMENTS which they “reverse” quickly so that at least one SCORING ELEMENT returns to approximately its original state would likely not be considered STRATEGIC but would still be in violation of G407 and result in a VERBAL WARNING.

Opposing ALLIANCE NECTAR is a SCORING ELEMENT and would not change the considerations for G407, but if CONTROLLED would also be a violation of G408.

## Q23: Control vs momentary control consistency with scoring elements
<!-- qa:23 modified:1791917320000 answer:1791917320000 -->

- Status: answered
- Manual section: 11.4.3 | Rule: G407
- Asked: 2026-10-11 | Answered: 2026-10-13
- Link: https://game-qa.firstinspires.org/boards/2027/FTC/QA/qa/23

**Question**

Clarify Control and Momentary Control and game elemets. G407 can not CONTROL more than 4 elements. The explanation states "Examples of actions that are likely to not be perceived as STRATEGIC ...ROBOT MOMENTARILY CONTROLS 5 SCORING ELEMENTS which they “reverse” quickly so that at least one SCORING ELEMENT..." From G407, Momentary Control of more than 4 is okay. In responses to questions on this same action related G408 and opponents Nectar, Q&A answers have any CONTROL is a penatly. Why are Control and Momentary Control being used differently for different elements and different rules?

**Answer**

The specific language of G407 and G408 do not provide an explicit "MOMENTARY exception" to either rule. The violation of both rules is: "VERBAL WARNING. MAJOR FOUL and YELLOW CARD per MATCH, if STRATEGIC."

The Orange Box of G407 indicates that an isolated incident of a ROBOT MOMENTARILY CONTROLLING 5 SCORING ELEMENTS would likely to not be perceived as STRATEGIC. Per Section 1.7.1: "While orange boxes are part of the manual, they do not carry the weight of the actual rule (if there is an inadvertent conflict between a rule and its orange box, the rule supersedes the language in the orange box)."

However, a ROBOT that routinely MOMENTARILY CONTROLS more than 4 SCORING ELEMENTS or opponent NECTAR as part of the ROBOT design and/or strategy is likely to be perceived as deliberately violating G407 and/or G408 in order to gain a competitive advantage with foreseeable violations of one or both rules -- this is likely to be perceived as STRATEGIC violations by REFEREES.
